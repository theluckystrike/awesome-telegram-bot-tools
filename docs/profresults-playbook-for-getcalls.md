# What Arno Wingen built with ProfResults, and how to replicate it for getcalls.io

Assessment date: 2026-09-18

## 0. How to read this document

- **Scope.** Everything publicly visible about Arno Wingen's agency ProfResults (profresults.com): the founder story, the offer, pricing, guarantee, funnel pages, lead magnets, content engine, distribution, team, and the teaching program (Business in a Box) he built around the agency. Then a concrete replication plan for getcalls.io.
- **Evidence quality.** profresults.com and getcalls.io could not be opened directly from this environment (network policy). Facts about ProfResults pages come from search-engine extracts of those pages plus third-party sources (Clutch, Crunchbase, Vastgoed Actueel, Google Books, podcast listings, X). Each fact below is tagged with its source in the Sources section. Where a detail comes from Arno's course material as circulated by students rather than a page I could verify, it is marked **(curriculum, unverified)**.
- **getcalls.io.** The domain resolves (Cloudflare) but is not indexed by search engines, so the site's current content is unknown to me. The plan assumes getcalls.io sells **inbound phone calls / booked jobs for local service businesses** (trades, home services, clinics, etc.). If the product is different, the mechanics below still hold; only the unit of value ("calls") changes.

## 2. The business: what ProfResults actually sells

### 2.1 Positioning
- One-liner used across the site: "an online marketing agency that becomes your marketing department on-demand."
- Clutch description: performance-focused agency helping local businesses generate consistent leads and booked appointments; PPC, Meta ads, local SEO; "measurable results over vanity metrics"; acts as an extended marketing team.
- Homepage title tag is stuffed for local search intent: "BEST Marketing Bureau Amsterdam | Prof Results | looking for an online marketing agency near me for SEO, Google Ads or local SEO? We put your businesses at the top of Google".
- Recurring promise: **exclusive leads** (as opposed to shared lead platforms), delivered through Google Ads, Meta ads and local SEO / Google Business Profile.

### 2.2 The service, as described on the site
1. Build a website that converts.
2. Get visitors to it (Google Ads search campaigns, Meta ads, local SEO / GBP top-3 ranking).
3. **Screen everyone who reaches out before they hit the client's inbox.**
4. Report; month-to-month; 30 days' notice; no long-term contracts; no cancellation fees.

### 2.3 Pricing and offer structure
- **Paid trial:** one-time, upfront, 30 days of service. Listed at $750 on one page and $997 on the book-a-call page (inconsistent; see section 5). No commitment, no retainer, no 12-month contract, does not auto-renew.
- **Conversion mechanic:** a week before the trial ends the client is offered the retainer. The client must actively say they want to continue. The retainer is cancellable monthly.
- **Guarantee, verbatim logic:** "We can guarantee results because we only work with businesses we are 100% sure we can get results for. Only businesses that have what it takes will be offered our core offer." The guarantee is a **qualification filter**, not a refund clause.

### 2.4 Sales process
- Entry points: "Free marketing analysis" form, "Book a call" page, free GBP audit, lead-magnet downloads, newsletter.
- Book-a-call copy (paraphrased from the page): if you're a good fit, Arno personally reviews your company and marketing, works out what he would do differently, and discusses it in depth on a call. No cost, no obligation. If you want to work together he tells you exactly how that works; if not, that's fine. "No hard selling, no pressure, no annoying sales tactics."
- Then the paid trial, then the retainer.

### 2.6 Lead magnets
- **"4 Easy Steps To Getting More Clients Using Meta Ads"** (PDF, email-gated, authored by Arno as founder of ProfResults). Opening argument: never in history has it been this easy to put a message in front of exactly the right people; most businesses just boost a post and never use Meta properly. The clone agency's version of the same guide lists the chapters as: why Meta is the best platform to reach your perfect customers; what most businesses get wrong; how to attract clients without spending a fortune; how to make your perfect clients read your ads; how to measure and optimise.
- Free GBP audit (3 quick wins).
- "First three things we do" video.
- Newsletter: "expert PPC and Meta advertising tips for local business owners... no fluff or theory, just proven strategies you can use immediately to bring more customers through your door."

### 2.7 Content engine (this is the part most people under-copy)
- Blog described as daily PPC and Meta insights for local businesses: case studies, how-tos, industry news.
- Emails are short and conversational, signed by the founder: "Hi, It's Arno" ... "Ran into something superweird" ... a story about a client's ads ... one lesson ... CTA.
- Known articles and their single idea:
  - **BMR:** local businesses win by doing ONE thing: be the most relevant person to call at the most relevant time. Example: almost no divorce attorney puts "divorce attorney" in the ad headline.
  - **Three Letters That Will Magically Get You More Clients:** the RSO, Rock Solid Offer. Kills "Call us today for excellent customer service and competitive pricing" as generic, invisible, does not register when a prospect scans headlines.
  - **Mass Marketing Doesn't Work. Do This Instead:** carpet-bombing your logo only works with a billion-dollar budget; an RSO that speaks to the ideal client also catches the people who are "thinking about it".
  - **Smart Small > Big Dumb:** big companies got dumb after critical mass; if you don't have a $600M budget, your ads must have ROI. Humorous "Schrödinger's P.S." sign-off.
  - **Stop randomly throwing things:** diagnose before you prescribe (medical analogy); before touching GBP, Google Ads or Meta, understand current performance.
  - **You really need to do this:** every local business should run Google Ads; new clients' accounts always show Performance Max performing "miserably"; start with plain search campaigns and test.
- Distribution of the same idea: X post (daily marketing critiques, e.g. a pigeon street sign with "no call to action, no branding, no response mechanism... this is the competition"), Facebook page videos ("Does this Google Ads thing work for any business?"), LinkedIn Pulse reposts by team members, newsletter.

**Assessment.** ProfResults is a direct-response funnel wrapped around a commodity service: authority content at the top, low-friction free analysis in the middle, a paid trial as the risk-reversal, and a qualification-based guarantee that protects margins. The mechanics are simple, public and copyable. What is not copyable without work is the volume and consistency of founder-voice content.

## 3. The replicable playbook he teaches around it (Business in a Box)

Public description: "a step-by-step framework for launching and scaling a six-figure agency or service business from zero." The following is the operating sequence as circulated by students of the program; it matches what ProfResults visibly does, but I could not verify the lesson text itself in this session. **(curriculum, unverified)**

1. **Pick a niche** you can reach, that can pay, and that has a lead problem you can solve. Local service businesses with high ticket sizes score best.
2. **Hit list of 25 prospects** per niche/city with owner name, email, phone, website, notes on their current marketing.
3. **One-page website** in the founder's name or a plain brand: headline about outcome ("more clients, guaranteed"), three reasons-why blocks (guarantee, results, specialisation), a "get in touch" form, a free marketing analysis CTA.
4. **Outreach.** Short plain-text email: found your business while looking for [niche] in [city]; I help [niche] attract more clients with effective marketing; would a quick call this week work? Follow up two or three times over about a week, then phone. Track everything in a spreadsheet or CRM.
5. **Sales call.** Qualify (what do you do, how do you get clients now, what have you tried, what would "working" look like), find the bottleneck, propose one clear solution, send a one-page proposal. Price as management fee plus client-funded ad budget.
6. **Fulfilment.** Meta or Google ads with a two-step structure: a lead magnet or free offer first, then retargeting to the booking step. Simple landing page, lead form, screening call or CRM automation, weekly reporting.
7. **Content in a Box.** One article per week from the founder, repurposed into the newsletter and social posts. Same voice, one idea per piece, always a CTA to the free analysis.
8. **Marketing Mastery basics** baked into every asset: one audience, one message; headline says what they want; WIIFM; measurable response mechanism; cut the fluff.

## 5. What to copy, what to improve, what to avoid

**Copy as-is**
- The paid trial as risk reversal with a forced opt-in to the retainer. It filters tyre-kickers and makes the retainer conversation happen while results are fresh.
- The qualification-based guarantee. It lets you say "guaranteed" without underwriting bad-fit clients.
- The "no hard selling, no pressure" book-a-call page. It sells by refusing to sell.
- Vertical and city landing pages built from one template.
- Lead magnet + retargeting + newsletter as the top of funnel.
- One-idea articles in a founder voice, repurposed everywhere.
- Screening what reaches the client. For a calls business this is the product.

**Improve**
- **One price.** ProfResults shows $750 on one page and $997 on another. Pick one, show it everywhere.
- **Page titles.** ProfResults' offer pages carry a generic "Online Marketing Agency" title and the homepage title is keyword-stuffed. Use outcome titles per page ("Exclusive calls for plumbers in [city] | getcalls").
- **Proof on the page.** Arno relies on personal authority. getcalls.io should lead with call recordings (with permission), call counts per client per month, and one named case study with numbers, the way the clone agency did with "Xpress Drywall and Taping: from no online presence to $80,000 in new sales via Google and Facebook ads".
- **Reporting as marketing.** Weekly "calls delivered" report screenshot is the retention tool and a social post.

**Avoid**
- Building the brand on a controversial community. Arno's audience comes largely through The Real World; that is not transferable and carries reputational risk in many markets. Build the founder voice on the operating story instead.
- Founder-only dependency. Arno's funnel collapses without Arno's daily writing. Batch content and bring in a second voice by month 3.
- Brand copy-cats. Because Arno teaches the template publicly, near-identical agencies use the ProfResults name (section 6). Register the getcalls brand and lock the obvious domain variants early.

- Promises: exclusive, high-quality leads; simple pricing; results you can see; full access to ad accounts and reports; month-to-month; the identical "we only work with people we know we can get results for" guarantee.
- Case study: Xpress Drywall and Taping, no online presence to $80,000+ in new sales through Google and Facebook ads, "#1 local choice on Google".

## 7. Replication plan for getcalls.io

### 7.1 Positioning and offer (week 1)
- **Niche first.** Start with one or two high-ticket local niches where a call is worth $300+ (HVAC, plumbing, roofing, water damage, dental implants, personal injury). Arno's rule: relevance beats reach.
- **Headline options** (test on the homepage):
  1. "More calls from customers who are ready to book. Guaranteed."
  2. "Exclusive calls for [niche]. Not shared leads. Not tyre-kickers."
  3. "We make your phone ring. You answer it. That's the whole deal."
- **Rock Solid Offer:** "30-day paid trial: [N] qualified calls for $[price], tracked and recorded, or we keep working free until you get them. No contract after that, month to month, 30 days' notice."
- **Guarantee page copy:** "We can guarantee calls because we only take on businesses we are certain we can get calls for. If we don't think we can, we'll say so on the first call."

### 7.2 Site map (week 1 to 2)
```
/                         hero, 3 pillars (exclusive calls, screened, guaranteed), how it works, proof, CTA
/free-call-audit          form: name, business, niche, city, website, phone, monthly calls now, biggest challenge
/book-a-call              Arno-style "no hard selling" page + calendar
/offer/[niche]            one template per niche with niche vocabulary and proof
/[city]                   local landing page (top-3 maps stat, process, CTA)
/guide                    "4 Easy Steps To Getting More Calls" PDF, email gate, thank-you page with booking CTA
/newsletter               single field sign-up, promise: short, practical, 3x/week
/blog                     one idea per post, founder byline
```

### 7.3 Lead magnets (week 2)
1. PDF: "4 Easy Steps To Getting More Calls From Google and Meta" (pick the right searcher, make the call the only CTA, screen the call, retarget the ones who didn't call).
2. Free call audit with a 3-point fix list and a Loom.
3. Video: "The first three things we do to make a [niche]'s phone ring in 30 days."

### 7.4 Outreach machine (from week 2, every day)
- Hit list: 25 prospects per niche per city, refreshed weekly.
- Email 1 (day 1): "Hi [Name], found [Business] while looking for [niche] in [City]. I help [niche] get more calls from customers ready to book, without shared leads. Worth a quick call this week to see if it fits?"
- Follow-ups day 3 and day 6 (one line each), phone call day 8.
- Sales call flow: qualify (how calls come in today, how many are junk, what a good month looks like), find the bottleneck, PAS, propose the trial, send a one-page proposal the same day.
- Targets: 10 new contacts/day, 3 to 5 calls/week, 1 trial/week by month 2.

### 7.5 Content engine (from week 2)
- One article per week, founder byline, one idea each. Seed list, reframed from Arno's posts:
  - "Be the most relevant number at the most relevant moment" (BMR).
  - "Your offer is the ad" (RSO).
  - "Mass marketing is for billion-dollar brands; you need response."
  - "Diagnose before you spend: the 10-minute call audit."
  - "Why Performance Max eats your budget and what to run instead."
  - "Shared leads vs exclusive calls: the real cost per booked job."
- Repurpose: newsletter (3x/week), 5 social posts, one short video.

### 7.6 Paid acquisition for getcalls.io itself (month 2)
- Meta two-step: guide download ad to niche owners in target cities, then retarget downloaders with the free call audit, then with the book-a-call page. Small daily budget; the point is proof of the method you sell.

### 7.7 Fulfilment stack for clients (month 1 onward)
- Google Ads search campaigns (call-only and call extensions), Meta lead ads for the retarget layer, tracked numbers per client, call recording and scoring, GBP optimisation, weekly "calls delivered" report.
- Screen calls: score every call (booked, quote, junk), report only qualified. This is the product's proof and the retention lever.

### 7.8 Metrics to run the business on
| Stage | Metric | Target to aim for |
|---|---|---|
| Outreach | reply rate, calls booked per 100 emails | 8 to 12%, 3 to 5 |
| Sales | call to trial conversion | 25 to 40% |
| Trial | trial to retainer conversion | 60%+ |
| Fulfilment | cost per qualified call, calls per client per month | niche-dependent; set per offer page |
| Content | newsletter growth, audit requests per week | 50+/week subs by month 3, 5+ audits/week |

### 7.9 90-day sequence
- **Days 1 to 14:** niche, offer, one-page site, free call audit, book-a-call page, hit list, first 100 emails, PDF drafted.
- **Days 15 to 45:** daily outreach, first trials, weekly article + newsletter, first case study captured (recordings, counts, revenue).
- **Days 46 to 90:** two niche pages and two city pages live, retargeting on, first retainer conversions, hire or contract a closer, publish the case study with numbers.
